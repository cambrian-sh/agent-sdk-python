<!-- section: propose-memory-note -->
Save a durable note to the principal's memory notes (personal or org). op append adds content; replace_section and remove_section change one section by its heading. Keep notes short and factual: stable preferences, conventions and facts that will matter in later runs, never task scratch work. The write is kept at once but appears in your notes only from the next run. A refused write comes back with a reason; do not retry it unchanged.

<!-- section: propose-skill -->
Propose a reusable skill: a full SKILL.md whose frontmatter name equals name, with a rationale and optional evidence cids. base_version is empty for a new skill, else the version you are revising. The skill stays inactive until an operator approves it, so do not rely on it in this run. A refused proposal comes back with a reason.

<!-- section: propose-memory-note-text -->
## Memory notes
- `{"action": "propose_memory_note", "scope": "personal"|"org", "op": "append"|"replace_section"|"remove_section", "section": "<heading>", "content": "<note>", "reason": "<why>"}` — save a durable note for later runs.
  Example: `{"action": "propose_memory_note", "scope": "personal", "op": "append", "section": "Preferences", "content": "Prefers metric units.", "reason": "stated by the user"}`

<!-- section: propose-memory-note-native -->
## Memory notes
- Call the `propose_memory_note` tool to save a durable note for later runs; it reaches your notes from the next run.

<!-- section: propose-skill-text -->
## Skill proposals
- `{"action": "propose_skill", "name": "<skill name>", "skill_md": "<full SKILL.md>", "rationale": "<why>", "base_version": ""}` — propose a reusable skill. It stays inactive until an operator approves it.

<!-- section: propose-skill-native -->
## Skill proposals
- Call the `propose_skill` tool to propose a reusable skill. It stays inactive until an operator approves it.

<!-- section: schedule-task -->
Set up a recurring task for the person you are working for, when they ask for something to happen on a schedule ("every Monday at 8, summarise ..."). goal is what to do at each run, written as their request; give the schedule as cron or rrule, and a timezone when they named one. They are asked to approve it first and nothing is created unless they do; once approved it runs unattended as them and each result lands in their conversation (delivery notify also notifies them; email asks the run to email them through their own mail account). Tell them what you proposed and whether it was created. A refusal comes back with a reason (invalid, limit, too_frequent, denied, no_decision); do not retry it unchanged.

<!-- section: manage-schedules -->
List, pause, resume or delete the recurring tasks of the person you are working for. op list returns their tasks with ids; pause, resume and delete take the id. You can only ever see and change their own tasks.

<!-- section: schedule-task-text -->
## Recurring tasks
- `{"action": "schedule_task", "goal": "<what to do each run>", "cron": "<cron>"|"rrule": "<rrule>", "timezone": "<IANA zone>", "title": "<short name>", "delivery": "conversation"|"notify"|"email"}` — propose a recurring task for the person you serve; it is created only if they approve it.
  Example: `{"action": "schedule_task", "goal": "Summarise my inbox notes into Documents/weekly-summary.md", "cron": "0 8 * * 1", "timezone": "Europe/Berlin", "title": "Weekly summary"}`

<!-- section: schedule-task-native -->
## Recurring tasks
- Call the `schedule_task` tool when the person asks for something to happen on a schedule. It is created only after they approve it; report the outcome.

<!-- section: manage-schedules-text -->
## Managing recurring tasks
- `{"action": "manage_schedules", "op": "list"|"pause"|"resume"|"delete", "id": "<task id>"}` — list the person's recurring tasks, or pause, resume or delete one by id.

<!-- section: manage-schedules-native -->
## Managing recurring tasks
- Call the `manage_schedules` tool to list, pause, resume or delete the person's recurring tasks.

<!-- section: propose-knowledge -->
Propose a structured fact for the knowledge records: kind, entity_id and its values (each a predicate, a type and a value), with the evidence it rests on. It is stored as a proposal from you, never as the committed record; set request_commit to ask the person you work for to approve it, and only their approval makes it the committed value. Instead of an item you may propose a link (relation, from_ref, to_ref, evidence_id), which stays a candidate until a person confirms it. A refusal comes back with the rule it broke (an unknown kind, a value outside the allowed set, a state change the kind does not allow); do not retry it unchanged.

<!-- section: propose-knowledge-text -->
## Knowledge proposals
- `{"action": "propose_knowledge", "kind": "<kind>", "entity_id": "<entity>", "values": [{"predicate": "<name>", "type": "text"|"number"|"date"|"entity", "value": <value>}], "request_commit": true|false, "evidence_id": "<evidence>", "reason": "<why>"}` — propose a fact; it is committed only if the person approves it.
  Example: `{"action": "propose_knowledge", "kind": "decision", "entity_id": "decision/pricing-2027", "values": [{"predicate": "status", "type": "text", "value": "decided"}], "request_commit": true, "reason": "agreed in the 3 Nov meeting"}`

<!-- section: propose-knowledge-native -->
## Knowledge proposals
- Call the `propose_knowledge` tool to propose a structured fact or a candidate link. It is a proposal until the person approves it; report the outcome.

<!-- section: propose-ontology-change -->
Draft a change to what the knowledge records accept: kinds (each with its predicates, their types and allowed values) and relations, given as {"kinds": [...], "relations": [...]}. Each spec you give is added, or replaces the namespace's spec of the same name, so give the whole spec. You only draft it: a person reviews it and, if they approve, it applies as their change. A change that would alter the meaning of existing records (a type change, a removed predicate) is refused with the reason, as is one that changes nothing; do not retry it unchanged.

<!-- section: propose-ontology-change-text -->
## Ontology drafts
- `{"action": "propose_ontology_change", "change": {"kinds": [{"kind": "<kind>", "predicates": {"<name>": {"type": "text"|"number"|"date"|"entity", "enum": ["<value>"]}}}], "relations": [{"name": "<verb>", "family": "relation"}]}, "namespace": "<namespace>", "reason": "<why>"}` — draft a change to the declarations; it applies only if a person approves it.
  Example: `{"action": "propose_ontology_change", "change": {"kinds": [{"kind": "shipment", "predicates": {"carrier": {"type": "text"}, "status": {"type": "text", "enum": ["in_transit", "delivered", "lost"]}}}]}, "reason": "carriers now report shipments"}`

<!-- section: propose-ontology-change-native -->
## Ontology drafts
- Call the `propose_ontology_change` tool to draft a change to the kind and relation declarations. It applies only if a person approves it; report the outcome.

<!-- section: ask-person -->
Stop and ask the person who made the request a question you cannot answer from the request, your tools or what you have found (which of two customers they meant, a missing date). The run ends with the questions; their answer starts the next request. Ask only what you need, and only when guessing would risk the wrong result.

<!-- section: ask-person-text -->
## Asking the person
- `{"action": "ask_person", "questions": ["<question>"], "reason": "<why you cannot go on without it>"}` — end the run with questions for the person, when the work cannot go on without their answer.
  Example: `{"action": "ask_person", "questions": ["Two customers are called ACME: ACME Ltd (CUS-002) or ACME GmbH (CUS-019)?"], "reason": "the request names ACME"}`

<!-- section: ask-person-native -->
## Asking the person
- Call the `ask_person` tool to end the run with questions for the person, only when the work cannot go on without their answer.
